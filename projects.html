MPP Capstone: Southeast Asian Deportation Analysis

UC Berkeley · AAPI Force

Report available in the Portfolio section.

For my capstone, I investigated who the United States is actually deporting from the Southeast Asian refugee community: Cambodian, Laotian, and Vietnamese nationals whose presence in the U.S. is itself a consequence of decades of American military engagement in the region. Drawing on four large administrative datasets covering ICE arrests, detentions, removals, and immigration court proceedings, supplemented by interviews with directly impacted individuals and advocacy organizations, I documented an unprecedented intensification of enforcement against this community under the current administration. I found that removals reached a 17-fold increase over the prior baseline, that 41 percent of executed orders were at least fifteen years old, and that the population being removed bears little resemblance to the "worst of the worst" rhetoric used to justify it. The report pairs quantitative analysis with the lived histories behind the numbers, and closes with policy recommendations at the state, federal, and structural levels.

Homelessness Policy Research

UC Berkeley · Western Regional Advocacy Project · Co-author

Report available in the Portfolio section.

As part of a team with the Western Regional Advocacy Project, I helped assess how H.R. 1 might affect homelessness by examining the lessons of past federal policy. We traced the housing and homelessness responses of the 2000s, from the subprime crisis to the HEARTH Act, to understand how shifts in affordability, foreclosure, and federal frameworks shaped today's trends. I was a co-author on the resulting report, which synthesized our findings and presented recommendations to stakeholders.

Cross-Country Study on Culture & Economic Incentives

UC Berkeley · Independent Study

For this independent research project, I built the full quantitative backbone of a cross-country study on how cultural factors relate to economic incentives. I consolidated data from numerous published sources and global datasets, including the World Management Survey, cultural-distance and tightness measures, labor-supply elasticities, and World Bank indicators, into a single harmonized panel spanning 156 countries and over two decades. The work involved extensive data cleaning, recoding, handling and flagging of missing values, and merging mismatched country codes and years into a consistent structure. I then ran the statistical analysis in R, using fixed-effects and robust regressions with clustered standard errors, interaction terms, and sensitivity checks, and produced publication-ready regression tables and longitudinal visualizations. The entire pipeline was scripted as a reproducible R Markdown workflow.

Evaluation Framework for Education in Somaliland

UC Berkeley · Graduate Student Consultant

Working with a team of graduate consultants, I helped design a monitoring and evaluation framework for Kaabe Schools, part of the Abaarso Network in Somaliland, to benchmark student performance against international standards while preserving the school's Montessori-aligned approach. My contributions centered on the survey analysis and the design of the assessment framework itself: I worked on analyzing teacher survey data on instructional practices and perceived student proficiency, and on building the monitoring and evaluation structure, including the practice-exam analysis, theory of change, key indicators, and evaluation methodology. The final report translated these findings into recommendations for teacher training, curriculum, and data systems suited to a low-resource, post-conflict context.

Jan 2022 – Oct 2022

MSc Thesis: Media & Gender Inequality in Pakistan

University of Manchester

Report available in the Portfolio section.

My master's dissertation examined the role of broadcast media in reinforcing gender inequality in Pakistan. Using thematic discourse analysis, I studied a full season of a popular primetime drama, transcribing and coding every episode in NVivo through a poststructuralist feminist lens, and surfaced recurring themes of patriarchy, restricted autonomy, objectification, and rigid gender roles. I paired this with a discourse analysis of popular review channels to gauge how audiences received those portrayals, an angle largely absent from existing Pakistani media research.

Field Research: Women's Political Participation in Uganda

University of Manchester

Report available in the Portfolio section.

For this fieldwork project, I was part of a research panel conducting in-depth interviews with public officials, educators, and civil society representatives on the forces shaping women's political participation in Uganda. I personally led two of the interviews and contributed to the questioning across the others. Drawing on critical mass theory, I authored an analytical essay examining the institutional and socio-cultural constraints on women's effective political participation.

Jan 2021 – Jul 2021

Undergraduate Thesis: Gender Disparity & the Pakistani Economy

NUST

For my undergraduate thesis, completed with a team of three, I led the technical and econometric side of the research. Using an Autoregressive Distributed Lag (ARDL) model, we examined the long-run relationship between gender disparity in education and key macroeconomic variables in Pakistan, testing whether closing the education gender gap is associated with stronger economic outcomes over time. My role centered on the quantitative work: preparing and structuring the time-series data, specifying and running the ARDL model, and interpreting the results to draw out their economic and policy implications. The analysis made the case that reducing gender disparity in education is not only a matter of equity but of measurable economic benefit, strengthening the argument for investment in girls' education as a driver of long-term growth.
